## Question and source-level finding

MACI v3.0.0 `MACI.signUp` calls `signUpPolicy.enforce(msg.sender, policyData)` and then inserts the MACI public key. Its policy sees the caller address and policy bytes, but not the public key as a parameter. The shipped `SemaphorePolicy` checks a proof message against that caller address. A common gasless relayer would therefore be the caller for every voter, and the policy would not bind the proof to the MACI public key. The upstream policy test uses `MockSemaphore`; it is not evidence of a genuine membership proof.

`SemaphoreMaciRegistration.sol` is an isolated candidate that verifies a Semaphore v4 proof whose message binds chain ID, wrapper, MACI target, election-specific group ID and the exact MACI public key. Its scope derives from the wrapper address and chain, so repeated group IDs do not create a stable cross-election nullifier. It checks `Semaphore.verifyProof` and keeps its **own** spent-nullifier map before calling MACI `signUp` in the same EVM transaction. Public `Semaphore.validateProof` can be called by anyone; sharing its spent set would permit a front-run to block registration. The wrapper implements MACI's policy interface and requires MACI to call it during that same transaction. The local probe sent an invalid MACI-key transaction and observed the wrapper nullifier unspent after rollback.

The group issuer must place at most one identity per eligible voter into an election-specific group. Semaphore alone cannot enforce honest issuance. The public MACI signup key, nullifier, event order, gas sponsor, issuer records, IP and timing remain potential links. The MACI coordinator can decrypt individual commands. No receipt-freeness or coercion-resistance claim follows from this design.
